# Utilitarianism Is Demanding, But Utilitarians Need Not Demand

A short philosophical note on the distinction
between moral prescription and moral demanding within act utilitarian ethics.


## Motivation

This project started as a response to the demandingness objection,
particularly as raised in discussions surrounding
Peter Singer's *Famine, Affluence, and Morality*.[^1]
While I find the internal utilitarian logic difficult to refute,
I am skeptical that labeling virtually every act of capital accumulation as evil
is itself a utility-maximizing act.
Perhaps people are discouraged by the high standard of morality
that they fail to even try to be better.
It is as if giving a struggling student a "Dunce" cap for not getting an A,
a response widely recognized as counterproductive to any future improvements.


## Current Outline

0. Utilitarianism is a highly demanding moral theory.
1. Moral prescription by a theory and moral demanding by an agent must be differentiated.
2. Whether moral demands are themselves utility-maximizing must be evaluated case-by-case with utilitarian principles.
3. Withholding demands is not necessarily hypocritical or negligent.
4. Open questions and future directions.
